About · how we check · privacy

About Golapada

Golapada is a free collection of interactive space-science labs. Each one starts with a few plain sentences for anyone, then lets you work things out yourself: launch a rocket, land on Mars, find tonight's nakshatra, steer a capsule home. The details for experts are one tap away. It is made in India, with India's own missions and history alongside the rest of the world's.

FreeNo fees, no sign-up, no adverts.
PrivateNo tracking, no cookies, no accounts. What you type stays on your device.
CheckedEvery lab compares its own numbers with published ones, and says where it is unsure.
Works offlineInstall it as an app, or download the offline copy for a school.

Who makes it

Golapada is an independent project from India. It is not part of ISRO, NASA, ESA or any other space agency, school board or company, and none of them has reviewed it.

The labs are written with the help of Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic. That makes it possible to build a lot quickly, and it is also exactly why every number is checked against a published source and every page shows its checks: an AI, like a person, can be confidently wrong. If you find a mistake, please tell us (see below).

How the numbers are checked

  • Worked out, not copied. Where possible a lab calculates its numbers live in your browser, from physics and from published orbital data, rather than quoting them.
  • Every science lab shows its checks. Open For experts on any science lab (the questionnaire and the Reading Test instead name their published sources at the foot of the page): a table compares the page's own results with published values (NASA, JPL, ESA, ISRO and peer-reviewed papers). Green means it agrees, amber means close, red means wrong.
  • Sources on every page. The small print at the foot of each lab lists where its numbers and dates come from, and when they were checked.
  • Checked by machine every day. After every change, and once a day, a robot opens every page in a real browser. It fails if a page crashes, if it scrolls sideways on a phone, if any check turns red, or if a page grows too big for a slow connection.
  • Live data is fetched fresh. Satellite orbits, asteroid lists and the year's launches are refreshed from public sources every day. Each lab says how old its data is.
  • Honest about limits. Each lab has a What the page leaves out note. Plans and future dates are marked as plans. Where sources disagree, the page says so.

The Kundli Lab calculates a birth chart precisely, but Golapada makes no predictions; the Reading Test explains why vague readings feel so personal.

Privacy, in plain words

  • No tracking. Golapada has no analytics, no advertising, no cookies of its own and no accounts.
  • Your birth details never leave your device. The Kundli Lab, the personality questionnaire and the quizzes all work entirely inside your browser. Nothing you type is sent anywhere.
  • Your location, only if you ask. Tonight's Sky and Satellite Watch can use your location when you press Use my location. Your phone asks first, and the position is used only on that page; it is not sent or stored.
  • Phone pointing in Tonight's Sky reads your phone's compass and tilt only while it is switched on, and only on your phone.
  • Saved on your device: if you install the app, your browser keeps copies of the pages so they work offline. Launch Watch keeps the launch list for 30 minutes so it does not ask the server too often. Each lab also notes, in your browser only, that you have opened it, so the guided paths on the home page can tick it off, the home page counts the days you visit in a row, remembers when you last came (to say what changed since), and keeps your sky-challenge ticks and question-of-the-day score, and if you enter your birthday for "Your birthday in space" it is kept there too, with a button to forget it. The Mars Colony Simulator keeps your game and the weekly challenges you have completed. If you choose a city or share your location for the home page's space-station card, that place is kept there too. You can clear all of this in your browser's settings.

Report a mistake

Spotted a wrong number, a broken button or a confusing sentence? Every lab has a Report a mistake link at the very bottom, which opens a short form with the page name already filled in. It needs a free GitHub account. Please say what is wrong, what it should be, and where you found the right value.

Report a mistake

Reports are public on GitHub, so please do not include personal details such as your birth date or address.

For teachers

See Golapada for Schools for which lab fits which NCERT chapter, ready lesson plans and an offline download, and Worksheets for printable practice with answer keys. Every lab ends with a five-question quiz that pairs can work through in class.
