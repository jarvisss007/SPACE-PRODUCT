In simple words

Every mission sent to explore our solar system, from Sputnik in 1957 to today: where it went, which nation sent it, and what it did first.

Try this first: Under All missions, press Firsts only. Then pick a nation, like India, from the All nations and agencies menu to see what it did first.

  • In 1959 Luna 2 became the first human-made object to reach the Moon, by crashing into it. Weeks later Luna 3 took the first pictures of the Moon's far side.
  • Mariner 4 was the first spacecraft to fly past Mars, and it took the first close-up picture of another planet.
  • India's Chandrayaan-3 made the first soft landing near the Moon's south pole.

Quiz: five questions

Answer each one by using the lab above, then check. Good for a class: pairs work through them, the teacher reveals the answers.

  1. What did Mariner 4 do first?

    Use the lab: Type Mariner 4 in the Search names and descriptions box and read its card.

    Show answer

    Launched on 28 November 1964, it made the first successful Mars flyby and took the first close-up image of another planet. Its picture is in Seen by the spacecraft as "First TV Image of Mars".

  2. Which Indian mission in the archive carries an "A first" badge, and what was the first?

    Use the lab: In the nation menu, pick India. Read the cards and look for the A first badge. You can also press Firsts only.

    Show answer

    India shows 5 missions. Chandrayaan-3 (launched 14 July 2023) has the badge: the first soft landing near the lunar South Pole. Chandrayaan-1 discovered water on the Moon, but its line does not use the word "first", so it gets no badge.

  3. Which years had the most launches in the archive?

    Use the lab: On the Launches by year chart, find the tallest columns of dots. Click near 1965 and read the note under the chart. Then click near 1967.

    Show answer

    1965 and 1967 each show 12 missions, the most of any year. Many of the dots in those columns are grey Moon missions.

  4. Mariner 10 is famous for the first Mercury flyby. Why does it appear under the Moon filter?

    Use the lab: Click the Moon filter and search Mariner 10. Read its description. Then read the footer about how destinations are sorted.

    Show answer

    Its line begins "Lunar and Venus flybys in addition to the First Mercury flyby". The footer says destinations are sorted automatically from names and descriptions, so a few may be misfiled. The Moon rule is checked first and the word "Lunar" matches, so it lands under Moon.

  5. HistoryIn 1959, which mission first reached the Moon's surface, and which first photographed its far side?

    Use the lab: Press Firsts only, then search Luna (or click the 1959 column on the timeline). Read the cards.

    Show answer

    Luna 2 (12 September 1959) was the first artificial object on the Moon, by impact. Luna 3 (4 October 1959) took the first images of the far side of the Moon. Both were Soviet, launched two years after Sputnik 1 (4 October 1957).
