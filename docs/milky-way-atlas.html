Quiz: five questions

Answer each one by using the lab above, then check. Good for a class: pairs work through them, the teacher reveals the answers.

  1. Which star is closest to the Sun, and how far away is it?

    Use the lab: Scroll to the table The 25 nearest stars and read row 1.

    Show answer

    Proxima Centauri, at 4.2465 light-years. Its class is M5.5Ve, a red dwarf.

  2. How long would different ships take to reach Proxima Centauri?

    Use the lab: In How long to get there, pick Proxima Centauri from the menu. Compare the Time at home column for each ship.

    Show answer

    Voyager 1 at 17 km/s: about 74,886 years. The Starshot sail at 20% of light speed: 21.2 years. The 1 g ship: 5.9 years at home but only 3.5 years for the crew, because of relativity.

  3. How wide is the view when you jump from the Milky Way button to the Local Group button?

    Use the lab: Click the zoom button Milky Way and read the big line Across this view. Then click Local Group and read it again.

    Show answer

    Milky Way: about 135,453 light-years across. Local Group: about 8.40 million light-years, roughly 60 times wider. Our galaxy is about 100,000 light-years across, and Andromeda is about 2.5 million light-years away.

  4. Most of our nearest stars cannot be seen without a telescope. Why?

    Use the lab: Look at the Class column of The 25 nearest stars and count the classes that start with M. Then click Nearest stars and read the note under the scale.

    Show answer

    14 of the 25 are class M red dwarfs. The note says most nearby stars are faint red dwarfs. They are small, cool and dim, so they are too faint to see with the naked eye even though they are close.

  5. HistoryWhich spacecraft has crossed the heliopause, and when?

    Use the lab: Click Planets, then drag the zoom slider slowly towards Oort cloud until a dashed circle with a label appears. The footer gives its distance.

    Show answer

    The label reads Voyager 1 crossed it in 2012. The footer says this was at 121.6 AU. Out there the solar wind gives way to interstellar space. No spacecraft has yet reached the Oort cloud.
