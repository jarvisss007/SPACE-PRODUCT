A famous psychology experiment you can take yourself

The Reading Test

Fortune-tellers, astrology apps and “personality readers” can feel uncannily accurate. This page gives you a personal reading in two minutes. Rate how well it describes you, and then see what is really going on.

    Now the truth

    Everyone who takes this test gets exactly the same reading.

    Your name, birthday and colour were never used. The statements were written in 1948. That year the psychologist Bertram Forer gave 39 of his students a personality test, then a week later handed each of them a “personal” description. Every student actually got the same text, which Forer had put together from a horoscope book bought at a newsstand. The students rated it, on average, 4.30 out of 5.

    Forer’s students, 19484.30

    One of Forer’s thirteen statements, about sexual adjustment, is left out here. The other twelve are his exact words.

    Why it works

    • The statements fit nearly everyone. Who doesn’t want to be liked, or sometimes doubt a decision?
    • “At times you are…, at other times…” covers both sides, so it is always true.
    • Mostly flattering. People accept praise as accurate more readily than criticism.
    • It feels made for you. Once you believe it was written about you, you fill in your own meaning.
    • Authority helps. People accept a reading more easily from someone who seems expert, and those who badly want approval are the easiest to convince.

    If a person seemed to know you

    Readers can also pick up a great deal in conversation without seeming to. Psychologists call this cold reading: watching your reactions, asking small questions that sound like statements, guessing broadly and letting you fill in the details, and remembering the hits while you forget the misses. In India newborns are traditionally named from their birth chart, so even a name can hint at the sky on the day someone was born.

    Three questions protect you:

    • Could this sentence be said about almost anyone?
    • Did I give away the answer, by a word, a nod or my face?
    • How many guesses were wrong, and did I forget them?
