From a church in Thumba to the Moon · every Indian orbital launch

ISRO Launches

In 1963 India launched its first rocket, a small American-built Nike-Apache, from Thumba near Thiruvananthapuram, where a church gave its prayer hall and bishop’s room to the new space station. Sixteen years later India tried to reach orbit with its own rocket and failed. This page lays every orbital launch since then on one chart: the failures, the comebacks, and what flies next.

How much each rocket lifts

The same rocket lifts far more to a low orbit than to the high transfer orbit used for communication satellites, or towards the Moon.

Published capacities: PSLV-XL 3,800 kg to low orbit, 1,750 kg to sun-synchronous orbit, 1,300 kg to GTO. GSLV 6,000 kg to low orbit, 2,500 kg to GTO. LVM3 10,000 kg to low orbit, 4,200 kg to GTO, 3,000 kg towards the Moon. SSLV up to 525 kg to low orbit. Try them in the Rocket Lab.

    Quiz: five questions

    Answer each one by using the lab above, then check. Good for a class: pairs work through them, the teacher reveals the answers.

    1. HistoryWhen did an Indian rocket first put a satellite into orbit, which rocket was it, and how heavy was the satellite?

      Use the lab: Tap the first green dot on the SLV-3 row of Every flight since 1979, or read The road from Thumba.

      Show answer

      18 July 1980, on SLV-3: the 35 kg Rohini RS-1. The first SLV-3 flight, in 1979, had failed.

    2. Which Indian rocket has never failed so far, and how many times has it flown?

      Use the lab: Read the right-hand ends of the lines in Learning to fly.

      Show answer

      LVM3: 100% over 9 flights, including Chandrayaan-2 and Chandrayaan-3.

    3. How many satellites did PSLV-C37 carry in 2017, and how does the page check that number?

      Use the lab: Search C37 in The launch log, then read How far to trust it.

      Show answer

      104 satellites. The page counts them from the payload list (one Cartosat-2D, the small satellites and 88 + 8 Doves and Lemurs), and the count matches the published record.

    4. A rocket can lift much less to a high orbit than to a low one. How much can LVM3 carry to low orbit and how much to GTO?

      Use the lab: Read the LVM3 bars in How much each rocket lifts.

      Show answer

      10,000 kg to low orbit but 4,200 kg to GTO, well under half. Climbing higher costs far more speed, so the payload must shrink.

    5. HistoryWhy was India’s first rocket station built at Thumba, and what did the local church give?

      Use the lab: Read the first entry of The road from Thumba.

      Show answer

      Thumba lies very close to Earth’s magnetic equator, ideal for studying the upper atmosphere. The church gave its prayer hall and the bishop’s room, and the first rocket, a Nike-Apache, flew on 21 November 1963.
