NASA’s DART · the rocket equation’s cousin · orbits that drift

Planetary Defense Lab

On 26 September 2022 NASA flew a 610 kg spacecraft into Dimorphos, a 151-metre moonlet circling the asteroid Didymos, at 22,530 km/h. It was the first time people deliberately moved a natural object in space. Nothing was threatening Earth; it was a test. Here the measurement is redone from the published orbit periods, and then you plan the defence against an asteroid that is.

In simple words

In 2022 NASA crashed a spacecraft called DART into a small asteroid and changed its path. This page shows how we could protect Earth.

Try this first: Press Replay the impact, then move the sliders to change the asteroid and the warning time.

  • A small push given many years early is enough to make an asteroid miss Earth.
  • With little warning, you need a much bigger push.
  • Finding dangerous asteroids early is the most important part of protecting Earth.

The hit, seen from above

The dashed circle is Dimorphos’s orbit before the impact; the bright loop is after. The real change is tiny, so the drawing makes it 4 times bigger. Ghost dots show where Dimorphos would have been without DART: the gap grows by about 33 minutes of orbit every lap.

Re-measuring the push

Astronomers timed the eclipses as Dimorphos passed in front of Didymos, before and after the hit. From those two periods alone, Kepler and Newton give how hard it was pushed.

Defend Earth

Now a real threat. An asteroid is found on course to hit Earth. You can send DART-class impactors. Pushing it early is what matters: a nudge of a few millimetres per second, given years to grow, moves the asteroid by thousands of kilometres. This is because changing an orbit’s speed changes how long each lap takes, and the shift adds up lap after lap.

Apophis, 13 April 2029

Apophis, about 340 m across, will pass 31,600 km above Earth’s surface on 13 April 2029, closer than the geostationary satellites. It will not hit. But it shows why a near miss is closer than it looks. Earth’s gravity bends incoming objects towards it, so the target Earth presents is wider than Earth itself.

Then and now

Tap a year to open it.

  1. 1908Tunguska

    On 30 June 1908 a space rock tens of metres across exploded in the air over Siberia. It flattened about 80 million trees across roughly 2,000 km², and nobody saw it coming.

  2. 1980What killed the dinosaurs

    Luis and Walter Alvarez, father and son, with Frank Asaro and Helen Michel, found too much iridium, a metal rare on Earth but common in asteroids, in rocks from the dinosaurs’ last days. They proposed that an asteroid about 10 km wide caused the mass extinction.

  3. 1994A comet hits Jupiter

    Between 16 and 22 July 1994, more than 20 pieces of comet Shoemaker–Levy 9 smashed into Jupiter, leaving dark scars in its clouds. People watched a real impact happen for the first time, and took the danger more seriously.

  4. 1998Spaceguard: the big search

    NASA was given the goal of finding 90% of near-Earth asteroids bigger than 1 km. Telescopes swept the sky, and the goal was reached at about the end of 2010.

  5. 2013Chelyabinsk

    On 15 February 2013 a rock about 20 m across exploded high above Chelyabinsk, Russia. The blast broke windows across the city and about 1,500 people needed medical help, mostly from flying glass.

  6. 2022DART moves an asteroid

    On 26 September 2022 NASA’s DART crashed into Dimorphos. Its lap around Didymos got about 33 minutes shorter, the first time people changed the path of a natural object in space.

  7. 2024India looks at ApophisIndia

    In mid-2024 ISRO chairman S. Somanath said India wants to join planetary defence and study Apophis during its 2029 close pass, either with its own spacecraft or with NASA, ESA and JAXA. How India takes part was still to be decided.

  8. 2024–252024 YR4’s scare

    Found in December 2024, this asteroid briefly had a 3.1% chance of hitting Earth in December 2032, the highest NASA had ever recorded for an object its size. New observations brought the risk close to zero by late February 2025.

  9. 2026Hera reaches Didymos

    Launched by ESA in October 2024, Hera arrived at Didymos in early November 2026, about a month earlier than first planned. It will measure DART’s crater and weigh Dimorphos, so the push can be turned into a recipe for the future.

  10. 2027NEO Surveyor

    NASA plans to launch this asteroid-hunting telescope no earlier than September 2027. Dark asteroids are hard to see in ordinary light but glow in infrared, which is what it looks for.

  11. 2029Apophis flies by

    On 13 April 2029 Apophis, about 340 m across, will pass about 32,000 km from Earth. It will not hit, but it is a rare chance to see a big asteroid up close.

For experts: Checks
For experts: What the model leaves out

Dimorphos’s orbit is treated as a circle and the push as pointing straight along its path. The published β of 2.2 to 4.9 also accounts for DART hitting at an angle, which is why the simple estimate here sits inside that range rather than on its centre. The defence planner uses the “three times the push times the time” rule, which holds for a push along the asteroid’s path on an orbit like Earth’s. A real campaign would also aim the push, use gravity assists, and watch for keyholes, the small regions near a planet that bend an orbit into a later hit.

Quiz: five questions

Answer each one by using the lab above, then check. Good for a class: pairs work through them, the teacher reveals the answers.

  1. By how much did DART shorten Dimorphos's lap around Didymos?

    Use the lab: Read Lap shortened by in the Re-measuring the push card.

    Show answer

    About 33.2 minutes: from 11 h 55 min 17 s to 11 h 22 min 3 s.

  2. With the starting settings, how many DART-class impactors are needed to save Earth?

    Use the lab: In Defend Earth, keep diameter 151 m, 10 years, β 3.6 and density 2,400 kg/m³. Raise Number of DART-class impactors one step at a time and watch the verdict.

    Show answer

    4. With 1 it Still hits (shift 2,986 km, 34% of what is needed). With 3 it is only Grazing (8,958 km against 8,710 km needed). With 4 it is Deflected (11,944 km).

  3. With one impactor, what does extra warning time do?

    Use the lab: Keep 1 impactor. Set Warning time before impact to 1 year and read Shift by impact day. Then slide it to 30 years.

    Show answer

    1 year: about 299 km, only 3% of what is needed. 30 years: about 8,958 km, and the verdict becomes Grazing. The shift is 3 × push × time, so 30 times the warning gives 30 times the shift.

  4. Why did Dimorphos lose more momentum than DART brought to it?

    Use the lab: Compare Momentum DART brought with Momentum Dimorphos lost in Re-measuring the push, and read the note below.

    Show answer

    DART brought about 3,818 kN·s, but Dimorphos lost about 11,758 kN·s, so β is about 3.1. The extra came from the recoil of rock blasted into space by the impact, like the kick of a gun.
