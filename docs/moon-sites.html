Moon · every landing · 1966 to now

Moon Landing Sites

Since 1966, five countries have landed spacecraft gently on the Moon: the Soviet Union, the USA, China, India and Japan. Twelve astronauts have walked there, robots have driven and drilled there, and lately companies have started trying too. Many attempts also crashed. This page puts every one of them on a single Moon you can turn with your finger, including the far side that never faces Earth. You can see why everyone is now heading south, and measure how far apart the sites are.

In simple words

Spacecraft have landed gently on the Moon 28 times since 1966, and many more have crashed. This page puts every landing on one Moon you can turn with your finger, including the far side we never see from Earth.

Try this first: Press Far side and tap the dot for Chang'e 4. Then press South pole, and under Measure the Moon press Chandrayaan-3 → Chang'e 6.

  • Only five countries have landed softly on the Moon: the Soviet Union, the USA, China, India and Japan. Twelve astronauts walked there between 1969 and 1972.
  • China's Chang'e 4 made the first far-side landing in 2019. In 2024 Chang'e 6 brought the first far-side rocks home.
  • Everyone is now heading for the south pole, because deep craters there that never see sunlight hold water ice. India's Chandrayaan-3 landed closer to it in 2023 than anyone before.

Measure the Moon

On a ball, the shortest path between two places is not a straight line on a flat map, it is part of a great circle, like a rubber band stretched tight around the ball. The page works it out with the formula below, using the Moon's average radius, R = 1,737.4 km. The path is drawn in gold on the globe.

h = sin²(Δlat/2) + cos(lat₁)·cos(lat₂)·sin²(Δlon/2)
distance = 2 · R · asin(√h)

Why the south pole now

The Moon's axis is tilted only about 1.5°, so near the poles the Sun always skims along the horizon. The floors of some deep craters there have not seen sunlight for billions of years. They are among the coldest places measured in the solar system, colder than −200 °C, so they can trap water as ice.

2009: Chandrayaan-1. NASA's Moon Mineralogy Mapper (M3), flying on India's first Moon mission, found the colour "fingerprint" of water and hydroxyl (a piece of a water molecule) spread thinly across the surface. The results were published in September 2009. A month later NASA's LCROSS probe slammed into the shadowed crater Cabeus near the south pole and found water in the plume it threw up.

2018: ice, directly. Scientists looked again at M3's data and found the fingerprint of real water ice sitting at the surface inside permanently shadowed craters, most of it near the south pole.

Why it matters. Ice can be melted for drinking water, and split into oxygen to breathe and hydrogen for rocket fuel. That could make the Moon a filling station instead of a place every drop has to be carried to. Some crater rims near the pole also get sunlight most of the time, which is good for solar power.

Artemis. NASA's Artemis II flew four astronauts around the Moon and back in April 2026, the first people near the Moon since 1972. NASA's current plan is for the first crewed landing, near the south pole, on Artemis IV, targeted for about 2028. That is why Chandrayaan-3, Luna 25, Odysseus, Athena and Chang'e 7 all aimed south. Press South pole on the globe to see how crowded it is getting.

Coming next

Nothing has landed on the Moon since Resilience crashed in June 2025. These are planned; Moon launch dates slip often, so treat every date as a hope.

  • 2026–27 · Griffin-1, IM-3, Blue Ghost 2Three American company landers. Griffin-1 (Astrobotic) is aimed near the south pole, IM-3 (Intuitive Machines) at the odd bright swirl Reiner Gamma, and Blue Ghost 2 (Firefly) at the far side.
  • 2027 · Chang'e 7China's mission to the rim of Shackleton crater, right at the south pole, to hunt for ice. It was due to launch in August 2026 but was postponed to 2027.
  • 2027 · Blue Moon MK1Blue Origin's first, uncrewed, lunar lander; it slipped from 2026.
  • About 2028 · Chandrayaan-4India's plan to bring Moon soil home from near Chandrayaan-3's landing site, using two LVM3 launches. Read the story of the last one in the Chandrayaan-3 Moon Lab.
  • About 2028 · Artemis IVNASA's current target for the first crewed landing since 1972, near the south pole.

Even at full screen, one pixel on this globe covers several kilometres of Moon, so every dot is far bigger than the uncertainty in its position. Distances use a perfect ball of radius 1,737.4 km; real paths over hills and craters would be a little longer. Dates are in UTC (world time). In local time, Chandrayaan-2's Vikram crashed on 7 September 2019 in India, SLIM landed on 20 January 2024 in Japan, Chang'e 6 landed on 2 June 2024 in China, and Resilience crashed on 6 June 2025 in Japan.

"Landed but tipped" is a judgement call: SLIM, Odysseus and Athena all touched down softly and sent back data, but did not end up standing as planned. Luna 23 landed but was damaged and could not do its job.

For experts: What the page leaves out

Only landers are shown. The page leaves out spacecraft that were meant to hit the Moon (like the Ranger probes, LCROSS, and India's own Moon Impact Probe, which carried India's flag to the surface on 14 November 2008), the many orbiters, and the separate final positions of the rovers (Lunokhods, Yutus, Pragyan), the Apollo moon buggies and the rocket stages that were crashed on purpose. Early failures are left out too: Luna 5, 7, 8, 15 and 18 and Surveyors 2 and 4 all crashed between 1965 and 1971. Small rovers that rode on these landers (SLIM's LEV-1 and LEV-2, Hakuto-R's and Athena's passengers) are mentioned only in words. The page shows no heights, slopes or real crater shapes, and the globe is lit evenly, not by today's real sunlight. It is not live: it was checked in November 2026.

Quiz: five questions

Answer each one by using the lab above, then check. Good for a class: pairs work through them, the teacher reveals the answers.

  1. Which spacecraft made the first landing on the far side of the Moon, and when?

    Use the lab: Press Far side on The landing globe and tap the green dots, or set Decade to 2010s.

    Show answer

    Chang'e 4 from China, on 3 January 2019, in Von Kármán crater. Because the far side never faces Earth, its messages travel through a relay satellite called Queqiao.

  2. How far is it across the Moon's surface from Chandrayaan-3 to Chang'e 6, and which way does the shortest path go?

    Use the lab: Under Measure the Moon, press Chandrayaan-3 → Chang'e 6.

    Show answer

    About 2,089 km. The shortest path passes close to the south pole (its furthest point is about 88° S), because the two sites are on opposite sides of the Moon.

  3. Which landing is the farthest south anyone has landed, and what went wrong?

    Use the lab: Press South pole on the globe and tap the gold rings, or find it in Every landing, in order.

    Show answer

    Athena (IM-2) from Intuitive Machines, at about 84.8° S on 6 March 2025. It ended up on its side in a small, cold, dark crater where its solar panels could not charge, and stopped working the next day.

  4. Why does everyone want to land near the Moon's south pole now?

    Use the lab: Read Why the south pole now.

    Show answer

    Some crater floors there have never seen sunlight and are colder than −200 °C, so they trap water ice. Chandrayaan-1's M3 instrument found signs of water in 2009, and found surface ice in shadowed craters in 2018. Ice could give drinking water, air to breathe and rocket fuel.

  5. HistoryWhich was the first spacecraft ever to land gently on the Moon, and what did it prove?

    Use the lab: Set Decade to 1960s and tap the first entry in Every landing, in order.

    Show answer

    The Soviet Union's Luna 9, on 3 February 1966, in the Ocean of Storms. Its photos showed the ground was firm enough to stand on, not deep dust that would swallow a spacecraft.
