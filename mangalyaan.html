Mangalyaan Mars Lab

On 24 September 2014 India’s Mars Orbiter Mission fired its engine for 23 minutes and was captured by Mars, making India the first Asian nation to reach Mars orbit. It cost about ₹450 crore (US$73 million), the cheapest Mars mission to date. ISRO published every burn: when, how long, and the orbit that resulted. This page rebuilds the whole voyage from those numbers and checks it against what really happened.

The trip in numbers

The path between Earth and Mars is solved exactly from the two real dates, leaving Earth on 30 November 2013 at 19:19 UTC and arriving at Mars on 24 September 2014 at 02:10 UTC, using JPL’s planet positions. Nothing is fitted.

Leaving Earth one step at a time

Like Chandrayaan-3, Mangalyaan could not be thrown straight at Mars. India’s PSLV rocket could only put it in a stretched Earth orbit, and the spacecraft’s own 440-newton engine raised that orbit in stages, always firing at the low point. Then came the burn that sent it to Mars, and 298 days later the one that braked it into Mars orbit. For each burn the model works out the push from ISRO’s before-and-after orbits, and from that push it predicts how long the engine had to fire.

One number is fitted: the engine’s efficiency (specific impulse). It is chosen so the propellant used across all the burns leaves the 40 kg ISRO reported in the tank after arriving at Mars. It comes out at , a little below the usual figure for this class of engine, because the model leaves out the small course corrections and the losses from firing for many minutes rather than in an instant. Every burn time is then a prediction.

Then and now

From the first blurry photos of Mars to India’s orbiter and what comes next. Tap a year to open it; green dots mark India’s part.

  1. 1965Mariner 4: the first close look

    On 14 July 1965 NASA’s Mariner 4 flew past Mars and sent back 22 pictures, the first close-up photos ever taken of another planet. They showed a dry world covered in craters.

  2. 1971Mars 3: the first landing

    On 2 December 1971 the Soviet Mars 3 lander became the first spacecraft to land softly on Mars. Its signal stopped after only about 15 to 20 seconds, and nobody knows for sure why.

  3. 1976Viking on the ground

    On 20 July 1976 NASA’s Viking 1 landed and sent back the first clear photo taken on the surface of Mars. The Viking landers also tested the soil for signs of life.

  4. 2003Japan’s Nozomi misses

    Japan’s Nozomi, launched in 1998, was meant to be the first Asian spacecraft to orbit Mars. After years of problems it reached Mars in December 2003 but could not brake into orbit, and flew on past.

  5. 2012India says yes to MarsIndia

    On 3 August 2012 India’s cabinet approved the Mars Orbiter Mission, and the Prime Minister announced it in his Independence Day speech on 15 August. That left ISRO only about 15 months to get ready for the November 2013 launch window.

  6. 2013Launch on the PSLVIndia

    On 5 November 2013 a PSLV rocket put Mangalyaan into a stretched orbit around Earth. Its own engine raised that orbit step by step, then on 30 November sent it off towards Mars: the journey you can replay at the top of this page.

  7. 2014Captured by Mars, first time luckyIndia

    On 24 September 2014 a 23-minute engine burn slowed Mangalyaan enough for Mars to capture it. India became the first Asian nation to reach Mars orbit, and the first country ever to get there on its very first try.

  8. 2014Cheaper than a space film?India

    The whole mission, rocket and ground stations included, cost about ₹450 crore. In 2014 Prime Minister Narendra Modi pointed out that this was less than the Hollywood space film Gravity, whose budget was reported at about US$100 million. It is a fun comparison more than a fair one: a film budget and a space mission budget are not counted the same way. But Mangalyaan really was the cheapest Mars mission yet.

  9. 2021Hope and Tianwen-1 arrive

    On 9 February 2021 the United Arab Emirates’ Hope probe went into orbit around Mars, the first Arab mission to do it. The next day China’s Tianwen-1 arrived too, and in May 2021 its rover Zhurong landed on the surface.

  10. 2022Goodbye, MangalyaanIndia

    Built to last about six months, Mangalyaan kept working around Mars for about seven and a half years. Contact was lost in April 2022 after a long eclipse left it without sunlight, and in October 2022 ISRO said the mission was over.

  11. 2030?Plan: Mangalyaan-2, with a landerIndia

    This is a plan, not a flight yet. ISRO’s chairman has said a second Mars mission is aimed at about 2030, with an orbiter and India’s first try at landing on Mars. Dates for missions like this often slip.

For experts: What the model leaves out

Every burn is treated as instantaneous. Long burns lose some push to gravity, which is why the real departure burn ran longer than predicted. The Earth orbit is assumed to keep a 252 km low point after the first burn, the figure ISRO gave then. The three small course corrections on the way (40.5 s, 16 s and 4 s on the small thrusters) are left out. The interplanetary path ignores the planets’ pull while the spacecraft is between them. The Mars arrival is worked out in a plane, while the real orbit was tilted about 150°, so the braking burn is an estimate.
