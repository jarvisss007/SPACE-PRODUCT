23 August · National Space Day

Space Day in a box

On 23 August 2023, Chandrayaan-3’s lander Vikram touched down near the Moon’s south pole. India now marks that day as National Space Day. This page is a ready-to-run 90-minute event for a school or club: a run sheet, a full-screen show mode that steps the room through four labs and three quiz rounds with a timer and team scores, and a certificate to print for everyone who took part.

In simple words

Everything a school or club needs to run a National Space Day event, held on 23 August. That is the day in 2023 when India's Vikram lander touched down near the Moon's south pole.

Try this first: Press Start show mode to put the event on the big screen. It steps the room through each lab and quiz round, with a timer and team scores.

  • The event takes an hour and a half and mixes four labs with three quiz rounds: land Vikram, escape a failing rocket in India's Gaganyaan capsule, read the live Sun and meet the old astronomer Aryabhata.
  • Short on time? Drop the third lab and its quiz round, and the event still works in an hour.
  • Under Certificates, type a name and print a certificate for everyone who took part. Names stay on your computer and nothing is sent anywhere.

What you need

One screen

A projector or big TV, and a laptop with internet. Students can follow along on phones in pairs, but they do not have to.

Teams

Split the room into two to six teams for the quiz rounds. Show mode keeps the score on screen; nothing is saved or sent anywhere.

No internet?

Download the offline copy beforehand. The labs work offline; live sky and Sun data need the internet.

The 90-minute run sheet

    Running short of time? Drop the third lab and its quiz round; the event still makes sense in 60 minutes. Each step has a timer in show mode, which only counts down: you decide when to move on.

    Certificates

    Type a name and press Print. The certificate prints alone on a white A4 page, landscape. Names stay on this computer: nothing is stored or sent.
