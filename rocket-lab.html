Rocket Lab

Start from a real rocket or design your own: pick engines from the catalogue, stack stages, load propellant, set the payload, and fly it. The simulator flies the ascent with thrust that changes with altitude, air drag, gravity and a gravity turn, then tells you what orbit you reached and how much push is left for the Moon or Mars.

How far to trust it

Before you rely on a number from a model, check it against something real. Here are the two rockets whose full stage data is published, flown by this simulator against what they actually did.

The simulator flies one fixed pitch program and simple guidance; real rockets fly trajectories optimised for each mission, which is worth several percent. For LVM3 we also had to assume the S200 booster’s sea-level performance and use its average thrust, and its upper stage pushes with less than half a g, where the steering a real flight computer does matters most. Treat results within about 10% of a goal as a coin toss.
